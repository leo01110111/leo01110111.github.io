2026-04-02: For the past three years, I have been thinking a lot about the fundamental questions in life. It bothered me that there isn't a meaning in life and yet we think there should be. It bothered me that somedays (especially after an all nighter) the world seemed unshakeably one way, but other days, it seemed like another. I don't think I will be thinking about these things anymore, because I stopped being bothered by them. I've learned to live lightly for the sake of my happiness, the most important reward. Too much philosophy cripples me. At some point, I just have to say, "Fuck it. I'll stop questioning what I know and start building with it." I don't think our idling ancestors survived natural selection. It were the ones that create who did.

Science and math only makes sense to me when I'm well rested. We say it's the truth, but more accurately it's the truth from a person who thinks like everybody else. If the whole world went mad, science and math will not exist. You might say, but an apple free falling from a tree would always accelerate at the rate of roughly \|9.81\| m/s^2 on Earth. But you're mad too! What makes you think the apple is even falling? Or that there's an apple? All order disappears.

There are only vibes in this world. We just call the vibes that have survived for a long time “truth”.

While I’m not sure if there’s actual truth in this world, I know I can be less wrong.

While I’m not sure if there’s a good life, I know there are good things and I should fill my life with them. I've also realized that the goodness of the really good stuff isn't subjective. My proof? The Beatles.

Advice of which I often get on podcasts, are heuristics. They're not always right. They are just approximations that have helped the advice giver act in a desirable way in their situation. Imagine life as a chess game. You don't know what sequence of moves leads to victory from the start, but it's easier to know how to make moves to control the center or take the opponent's pieces.

I have found that everybody has an interesting heuristic to share, if I can understand where they're coming from.

Heuristics aren't valuable unless you apply them, which is more difficult than collecting them.

Information comes in coarse grains, that is, they vary in precision and thus the context they assume. The general public on X follows a coarse grain. The Dwarkesh Podcast follows a coarse grain. Make sure you're at the right level. On a similar note, the medium in which you consume information matters. It's not just about the content. 

I like to think that the only true things in this world is math and science. They describe an objective world. However, most of our thoughts are subjective. Some times these subjective thoughts stress me out or make me angry. Remembering that they're not objective has added a lot of lightness in my life.

An example that may resonate easily with Chinese people: 
- Many Chinese parents expect good grades from their kids so much so that they would stop the child from developing interests that they think won't make money in the future. Growing up in a time of scarcity, the parents falsely believe that getting good grades is a must for a good life. Of course, that is nothing but a heuristic, not an objective fact. With that said, we must have heuristics to live well. All I'm saying is that understanding that they're not objective makes the burden lighter.

We like to think in absolutes whereas we should be thinking in terms of probabilities.
